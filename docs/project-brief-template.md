# Project Brief Template

## 1. What is this?
One sentence describing the project.

> *Example: "A browser-based stopwatch for speedrunning that tracks splits and compares them to personal bests."*

---

## 2. Who uses it and when?
Who is the user, and in what context do they use it?

> *Example: "Me, during a speedrun attempt, on a second monitor."*

---

## 3. What does it need to do?
Core features only. Describe what the **user does and sees** — not how it works under the hood.

- 
- 
- 

> *Example:*
> - *Start, stop, and reset a timer*
> - *Set a time for each segment when I reach it*
> - *See how each split compares to my personal best*

---

## 4. What does it NOT need to do?
Explicitly ruling things out prevents scope creep.

- 
- 

> *Example: No multiplayer, no accounts, no mobile layout.*

---

## 5. What does "done" look like?
Narrate a complete, successful use of the app from start to finish as if describing it to someone watching over your shoulder. If you can write this, you've answered most of the questions that would otherwise come up mid-build.

> *Example: "I open the app, press Start, complete the first segment, press Set Time, and the sidebar updates with my split time and whether I'm ahead or behind my best."*

---

## 6. Open questions / things I'm unsure about
Write down what you don't know yet. This helps you spot where some exploratory building is inevitable and sets expectations accordingly.

- 
- 

> *Example: "Not sure if I want cumulative time or split duration in the sidebar."*

---

## How to use this with AI

Paste the completed brief at the **start of the conversation** before asking for any code. The AI uses it as a reference for every decision it makes — layout choices, field names, edge case behaviour, all of it. You'll get far fewer surprises and far less back-and-forth.

The **"What does done look like"** section is the most valuable one. If you can narrate a complete use of the app, you've already answered most of the questions that would otherwise come up mid-build.
